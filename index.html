The north star

Not just a city.
A place to inhabit.

The ambition is the feeling of stepping into an anime: noticing a side street, meeting someone with a life of their own, climbing somewhere that looked interesting and returning to find that your actions mattered.

Cowboy Bebop informs the visual craft and atmosphere. Cyberpunk 2077 informs exploration, character encounters and consequential choices. Those are references, not assets to copy. Our characters, setting and story will be original.

The working title SKYLINE, the district scenario, the camera and the production engine are proposals. The core idea—an explorable 3D world with a continuously animated 2D-anime appearance—is the brief.

A target you can see

Drawn character.
Inhabited spaces.

The new board makes the idea tangible. Its traveler, architecture and story beats are exploratory. A strong image sets a target; it does not prove the game can render or animate it.

Proposed visual direction

From looking at a world
to living in it.

Grounded anatomy. Distinct silhouettes. Deliberately shaped shadows. Painted backgrounds with wear, repair and everyday life. Enough visual restraint to make people and paths readable.

The first attemptprototype

An idea meets its first wrong turn.

An early browser prototype used a side-view, 2D presentation. A launch failure was reported, and the later requirement made a larger mismatch clear: the game needed a genuinely explorable 3D world. The original was archived rather than erased.

Evidence & limits

Observed source mismatch; launch failure not independently reproduced.

READOUT-AX-001

What the research is changing

Learn the craft.
Learn the world.

Two separate lines of inquiry serve the same goal. They keep their own evidence and uncertainties, then meet in the design.

The anime track

How do drawings become a convincing, continuous experience?

  • History and project-specific studio practices, rather than one universal “anime pipeline.”
  • Storyboards, layouts, key animation, inbetweens, backgrounds, color, camera/compositing, editing and sound.
  • Faces, line work, shadow shapes and acting across changing views.
  • Original editable assets and what survives tool-to-tool handoffs.

A shader is one component. A face that looks good at one angle is not proof that it works as the camera moves.

The open-world track

How do places, systems and people make exploration worthwhile?

  • Movement, camera, spatial reading and world/mission integration.
  • Choices players can recognize, followed by understandable consequences.
  • Persistent state, recovery and what happens when a quest changes after a save.
  • Direct critic/player experiences, with platform, version and source limits retained.

Restoring a saved number is not proof that the whole world or a waiting quest resumes correctly.

Historical studio cost and scheduling explain choices; they do not set our creative ceiling. Actual quality, revision, compute and delivery limits still need measurement. More references or more code are not substitutes for observing the result.

The proposed first adventure

One neighborhood.
One meaningful change.

Reversible story experiment

A market street, a radio repair shop and a rooftop route. Meet a keeper. Investigate a failing relay. Decide which need to serve.

The proposal is deliberately small enough to inspect as a complete experience. Both outcomes need credible stakes, and the result should remain when you leave and return.

A story sketch, not a game demo

Select an alternative to explore the proposed consequence.

One relay. Two needs. The writing is still open: this is a way to test whether a choice can feel understandable and consequential.

Loop: explore · notice · meet · investigate · choose · see the response · return.

The next build milestone

Earn the small world.
Then grow it.

  1. See and play the exact current version

    Recover a permitted direct visual/play route, inspect identified motion references and capture a comparable baseline. The current browser-policy restriction is unresolved.

  2. Prove one character and one environment treatment in motion

    Review face, silhouette, acting, lines, shadows and background contrast as the view changes. Keep editable sources and compare an actual revision.

  3. Finish one complete quest loop

    Connect conversation, discovery, a visible consequence and reload behavior. Observe what a player understands separately from what they prefer.

  4. Validate the target phone

    Check launch, touch input, camera, orientation, frame behavior, persistence and both branches on actual hardware. Choose technology from measured needs.

  5. Expand what has earned its place

    Add encounters and connected spaces before city-scale systems. Combat, vehicles, crowds, progression breadth and full production scope remain open decisions.

Evidence we have

  • Preserved source and earlier prototypes.
  • Seventeen historical Godot physics/state checks and nine browser-core tests.
  • A user report that launch and movement work.
  • Selected reference-image inspection and separately documented technical/production research.

Evidence we still need

  • Accepted anime appearance in the running game.
  • Agent visual/play inspection of the latest graphics version and identified motion-reference review.
  • Complete interactive playthrough of both branches.
  • Actual phone performance and a demonstrated original asset/animation pipeline.

There is no verified release date. The next useful promise is an observed, short playable slice—not an unreviewed bigger city.
